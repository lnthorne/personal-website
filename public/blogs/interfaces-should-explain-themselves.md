## Clarity beats cleverness

The most useful interface copy answers three questions: **what is happening**, **what can I do next**, and **what will change if I do it?** Consistent labels and immediate feedback make complex flows feel calm.

A label does not need to be novel to be helpful. Familiar language lets people apply what they already know instead of learning a private vocabulary for every product.

## Use progressive disclosure

Show the essential decision first, then reveal supporting detail when it becomes relevant. This keeps the initial experience approachable without hiding the depth that experienced users need.

For example, a setup flow can lead with the information required to begin and place advanced configuration behind a clearly named option. The choice remains available without becoming a barrier for everyone else.

## Confirm outcomes, not clicks

A button press is not the outcome people care about. After an action, describe what changed: a draft was saved, an invitation was sent, or a report is ready to download. Feedback that closes the loop builds confidence in the system.
